Where basic science funding is heading: WA against the rest of Australia

Analysis, October 2026

Written from the October 2026 build. Complete rounds to 2024; 2025 dollars; per resident using each year's ABS population. The computed findings below refresh weekly; this text does not.

WA wins a third of the national rate of basic science funding per person, and the gap is widening.

  • Scale. In 2022–24 WA attracted about $11 per resident a year in ARC Discovery and NHMRC basic science funding, against $33 in the rest of Australia. WA has about 11% of the population but has won 3–6% of national basic science funding in every year since 2014.
  • Direction. Real funding per resident fell about 5.7% a year in WA over 2015–24, against 1.7% a year elsewhere. WA's share slid from 4.8% to 3.9% (three-year averages). Nationally basic science is shrinking in real per-person terms; in WA it is shrinking three times as fast.
  • Both funders. ARC Discovery fell 4.5% a year in WA while growing 0.8% a year elsewhere. NHMRC basic science fell 5.8% a year in WA and 1.4% elsewhere. The WA decline is not one scheme.
  • Fewer grants, not smaller ones. Across Australia there are fewer, larger basic science grants (count −3.5% a year, average size +3.3%). WA's count is falling twice as fast, −7.7% a year: 60–77 grants a year in 2014–18, 31–43 in 2022–24. Value per named investigator rose about 3.5% a year in both WA and elsewhere, so funded WA teams get similar-sized grants; WA is winning fewer of them. Single years are noisy at these numbers (2022 was a low point), but the direction is consistent.
  • Topics (per resident, 2020–24 against 2015–19).
    • WA grew in microbiology (+87%, from a low base), immunology (+13%) and ecology (+6%), while all three fell slightly elsewhere.
    • WA fell further than the rest of Australia in neuroscience (−20% against −12%), cell and molecular biology (−25% against −21%), physiology (−46% against −28%) and zoology (−21% against −15%).
    • Evolution fell about as much in WA as elsewhere (−6%).
    • Plant biology is the one area where WA is ahead per resident, at 1.6 times the rest of Australia. Neuroscience runs at about a fifth of the national rate per resident, and immunology about a seventh.
  • Institutions. UWA (about $102m in 2020–24, −7% nominal on the five years before) and Curtin ($37m, −30%) hold nearly all WA basic science funding. Murdoch and ECU grew from small bases.

Caveats. This covers ARC Discovery and NHMRC basic science only, not MRFF, CRC, block grants, WA Government or philanthropy. NHMRC data here starts with the 2014 round. A grant counts where its administering organisation is, so WA researchers on grants run from interstate count interstate. "Basic" for NHMRC relies on its Broad Research Area label, and the 2019 move to Ideas and Investigator grants changed the mix of schemes. The 2025 and 2026 rounds are left out until NHMRC outcomes are complete.

What block grants pay for

Research Training Program (RTP) funds research degree (PhD and research masters) students: living-allowance stipends, tuition fee offsets so domestic research students pay no fees, other allowances, and the university's costs of research training. It replaced the Australian Postgraduate Awards, International Postgraduate Research Scholarships and the Research Training Scheme in 2017.

Research Support Program (RSP) is flexible money for the indirect costs of research that competitive grants don't cover: libraries, labs and consumables, IT and data, research support staff, compliance, buildings and maintenance. It replaced Joint Research Engagement, Sustainable Research Excellence and the Research Infrastructure Block Grants in 2017.

How it is allocated: by formula, not by application. RSP follows each university's share of research income (HERDC). RTP follows research income and research degree completions. Block grants therefore track past competitive success with a lag of a couple of years. In 2021 the Government added a one-off $1 billion to RSP, which shows as a spike.

What isn't published: the Department publishes each university's allocation but not how each university spends it. Universities decide the split within the program guidelines. Department of Education: Research Block Grants.

Amounts are nominal dollars by allocation year. Pre-2017 schemes are grouped as "Earlier schemes".

Topics come from each grant's Field of Research code, plus keyword matches in titles and summaries (untick "Include keyword matches" for FoR codes only). A grant can sit in several topics, so topics don't add up to the total. See Data & methods for the rules.

Researchers are matched by surname and first initial across all funders. Two people with the same surname and initial will be merged; a name change splits one person in two. By default each grant's value is split equally among the people named on it; switch "Researcher credit" to full grant value to count the whole grant for everyone. NHMRC, MRFF and CRC name only the lead (CIA or lead partner), so their grants aren't split. Links next to a name are searches, not verified profiles.

Hand-curated from annual reports, media releases and government announcements. Coverage is partial: philanthropic and state funding has no national dataset. Each row links to its source.

Methods

  • Amounts are the total awarded value of each grant (current value for ARC), counted in the grant's commencement or application year, in nominal dollars. They are not annual expenditure.
  • State is the state of the administering organisation, which is where the money is managed, not necessarily where all research happens.
  • Basic science: ARC Discovery program schemes (DP, DE, FT, FL, IN) and NHMRC grants classed in the Basic Science broad research area. MRFF (mission-directed) and CRC / CRC-P (industry-led) are not counted as basic.
  • CRC rows are Commonwealth grant funding to Cooperative Research Centres and CRC Projects, credited to the lead organisation. Participant cash and in-kind contributions are not included.
  • Field is derived from the primary Field of Research (FoR) division, 2008 or 2020 codes. Natural sciences: 01–07, 30, 31, 34, 37, 41, 49, 51. Engineering & IT: 08–10, 40, 46. Health & medical: 11, 32, 42. NHMRC and MRFF grants without a FoR code are classed Health & medical.
  • Biology topics come from the grant's primary FoR code (2008 and 2020 codes), plus keyword matches in the title and, where published, the summary, keywords or description. Neuroscience: 1109, 3209, 5202, 170101, 170112, 170205. Evolution: 0603, 3104. Ecology: 0602, 3103. Genetics & genomics: 0604, 3105, 1103. Cell & molecular: 0601, 3101, 1101, 3205. Microbiology: 0605, 3107, 1108. Plant biology: 0607, 3108. Zoology: 0608, 3109. Physiology: 0606, 1116, 3208. Immunology: 1107, 3204. Bioinformatics: 3102 and related. Structural biology & biophysics: keywords only. Keyword lists are in etl/normalise.py. Keyword matching catches grants filed under another FoR (e.g. an evolution project coded as genetics) but also some clinical grants (e.g. "brain cancer" under neuroscience).
  • Researcher links (Institution, Google Scholar, LinkedIn, ORCID) open searches by name, not verified profiles, so check you have the right person. The institution search uses the organisation's website where known; co-investigators are searched by name only because the data doesn't say where they work.
  • Researchers are matched on surname plus first initial after stripping titles. ARC grants list every chief investigator where the ARC API provides them; NHMRC, MRFF and CRC list the lead only. By default grant amounts are split equally among named investigators; with full credit, researcher totals overlap.
  • Per capita uses ABS estimated resident population (June) for each year, from the ABS Data API. If that fetch fails, it falls back to June 2024 population for every year. Totals over several years are divided by the average population over those years.
  • Real dollars on trend charts are converted with the ABS Consumer Price Index (all groups, eight capitals, annual average) to the latest year. Other charts and tables are nominal.
  • Australia excl. WA is the other seven states and territories combined, using the state of the administering organisation.
  • Per-investigator credit splits each grant's value equally among the distinct people named on it. ARC lists every chief investigator; NHMRC, MRFF and CRC publish only the lead, so their grants are split by one. That overstates those leads against ARC teams; tick ARC only in Funders for a like-for-like view.
  • Trends use complete years only: a year whose grant count is under 60% of the median of the three years before is treated as not yet fully announced and left out.
  • Organisation names are matched to canonical names via an alias table (data/manual/organisations.csv). ARC reports the current administering organisation only.
  • Block grants and HERDC income come from Department of Education spreadsheets, whose layouts change between years. Check the source status above for gaps.
Data: Australian Research Council, National Health and Medical Research Council, Department of Health and Aged Care, Department of Industry, Science and Resources, Department of Education, WA Government, organisation reports. Not affiliated with any of them.
